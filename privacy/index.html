Privacy

In short: this site sets no cookies, uses no tracking or analytics and has no contact form. It loads nothing from other servers: fonts and images come from the site itself. I run no server of my own. Data about you arises in only two places: at GitHub, when you open the site, and with me, when you send me an email.

Hosting on GitHub Pages

This site is hosted on GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub, Inc. in the USA, a Microsoft company.

When you open a page, GitHub processes your IP address and technical data such as browser, page and time. This is needed to deliver the page to you. Without your IP address no page can reach you. GitHub also stores the IP address in logs to keep its services secure, even if you are not signed in to GitHub. I do not get to see these logs.

The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. My legitimate interest is to offer this site reliably and securely without running a server myself.

GitHub may also process the data in the USA. For the USA there is an adequacy decision of the European Commission, the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (Implementing Decision (EU) 2023/1795). GitHub is certified under it.

GitHub does not give a fixed period for how long it keeps the logs. According to GitHub's privacy statement, this depends on the purpose and on legal obligations. Details are in GitHub's privacy statement and in the description of GitHub Pages.

Contact by email

When you send me an email, I process your email address, your name if you give it, and the content of your message. I use it only to answer you. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. My legitimate interest is to answer your request. If it is about your data protection rights, it is Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR.

The mailbox is with Outlook.com, a Microsoft service. Microsoft may also process emails in the USA and is also certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework.

I delete your email once your request is done and I no longer need it. Writing an email is voluntary.

Downloads and links

The download buttons lead to the releases on GitHub. When you click one, your browser loads the file directly from GitHub. GitHub sees your IP address and processes it under its own responsibility, according to its privacy statement. On GitHub I only see how often a file was downloaded in total, not by whom.

Other links also lead to GitHub or other sites. There the privacy rules of that provider apply.

The apps

StartQueue and DynaTones work on your device. They collect no usage data, need no account and pass nothing on to me or anyone else. They contact only GitHub: both to look for updates, which you can switch off, and DynaTones also for its gallery, only when you open it. GitHub then sees your IP address and the app's name and version, as with any download. Each app's privacy statement on GitHub has the details: StartQueue, DynaTones.

No automated decisions

There is no automated decision-making and no profiling under Art. 22 GDPR.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction of processing (Art. 18) and data portability (Art. 20). Write to esteemworks@outlook.com.

I keep no data about visitors of this site myself, only emails you send me. The site's logs are with GitHub. For those you can also contact GitHub directly.

Right to object

You can object at any time, on grounds relating to your particular situation, to processing based on Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (Art. 21 GDPR). Here this concerns the hosting and contact by email. I will then no longer process the data, unless I can demonstrate compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, or the processing serves the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims.
